# PalaGo, screen by screen

A walk through the working prototype as the four people who use it actually
see it: a passenger booking a seat, an operator running a company, an
administrator running the platform, and a driver at the door of a bus.

Every screenshot below was taken from the running app against the seeded
database — signed in, clicked through, nothing staged. They are regenerated by
one command, so a screen that changes and a picture that does not is a
discrepancy somebody will notice. See [Regenerating these](#regenerating-these)
at the end.

> **Every peso here is test data.** This build has no payment provider. Card,
> GCash, Maya and bank are simulated; the one exception is cash taken at a
> counter, which is real money changing hands and is recorded as such. See
> [payment-flow.md](payment-flow.md).

## Before you start

```bash
pnpm db:start && pnpm db:reset
```

```bash
pnpm functions:serve
```

```bash
pnpm web
```

Three terminals: the database, the Edge Functions (payment, boarding passes and
account provisioning all go through them), and the app on port 8090.

Sign in with any of the seeded accounts — all share the password
`PalawanGo2026`. The full list, and what each one is for, is in
[test-accounts.md](test-accounts.md).

| `operator@palago.test` | Cherry Bus's console |

| `driver@palago.test` | The crew app |

## 1. Passenger — booking a seat

### Signing in

<img src="screenshots/01-sign-in.png" width="320" alt="PalaGo sign-in screen" />

Passengers sign themselves up; everybody else is given an account by somebody
above them. The sign-up form here can only ever create a passenger — the
database trigger that makes the profile hard-codes the role and ignores
anything the client sends with it, so there is no way to register as an
operator or an administrator.

### Home

<img src="screenshots/02-home.png" width="320" alt="Passenger home screen with wallet, points and upcoming trips" />

Wallet balance and loyalty points are read from the database, not accumulated
in the app. Upcoming trips show the next two bookings. The bell carries an
unread count, and the count is in its accessibility label too — a badge is
never the only way to know something is waiting.

### Searching

<img src="screenshots/03-search.png" width="320" alt="Trip search: origin, destination, travel date and passenger count" />

Origin, destination, date, and how many people are travelling. A trip that
cannot seat the whole party is not a result.

<img src="screenshots/04-results.png" width="320" alt="Search results showing two Cherry Bus departures with fares and seats left" />

The filtering happens in the database, not over a downloaded list. Results
exclude anything a passenger could not actually board: a cancelled or withdrawn
departure, a coach that has been taken off the road, a route that has been
retired, an operator that has been deactivated, and anything already full.

### The trip

<img src="screenshots/05-trip-details.png" width="320" alt="Trip details: operator, times, duration, bus and seats available" />

### Who is travelling

<img src="screenshots/06-passenger-details.png" width="320" alt="Passenger details form: full name, passenger type, mobile and email" />

Names, not seats. **Passengers never choose a seat** — the system assigns them,
which is the rule the whole booking path is built around. Up to ten people can
travel on one booking, and they are booked in a single transaction: either
everybody gets a seat or nobody does, so a family is never split across two
buses by a half-finished checkout.

The passenger *type* here is a claim, not a discount. Selecting SENIOR does not
reduce the fare — a verified discount comes from an approved ID, which is
[a separate flow](#discounts).

### Paying

<img src="screenshots/07-payment.png" width="320" alt="Payment screen: seats reserved, ten-minute timer, amount to pay, and a wallet balance too low to cover it" />

Two things on this screen are worth reading twice.

**"Seats — Assigned after payment."** Capacity is held for ten minutes, but the
actual seat numbers are not handed out until the money has cleared. That is
deliberate: it is what stops an abandoned checkout sitting on seat 12A while
somebody who has actually paid is turned away.

**The countdown is real.** When it runs out the hold is released and the seats
go back on sale. Nothing about it is decided by the app — the expiry is on the
row in the database.

The wallet option is offered but disabled, with the shortfall spelled out
(`₱210.00 · ₱490.00 short`) rather than a greyed-out button that does not say
why.

Paying by QR opens a payment page in a browser with no PalaGo session — the
token in the QR is the entire credential, and the page is deliberately narrow:
it can see the trip, the amount and the status, and nothing else about the
passenger or their other bookings.

### The ticket

<img src="screenshots/08-tickets.png" width="320" alt="My tickets: upcoming and past bookings" />

<img src="screenshots/09-boarding-pass.png" width="320" alt="Boarding pass with its QR code and seat" />

The QR is issued only after payment, and it is signed server-side. The app
cannot mint one, and a scanner cannot be talked into accepting one it did not
verify — the signature is checked at the door against the trip being boarded,
not just for being well-formed.

### Wallet and rewards

<img src="screenshots/10-wallet.png" width="320" alt="Wallet balance and transaction ledger" />
<img src="screenshots/11-rewards.png" width="320" alt="Loyalty points balance and reward catalogue" />

The balance and its ledger are written together or not at all. Points are
awarded when a trip is **completed**, not when it is paid for — paying is not
travelling.

### Tracking, emergencies, discounts, notifications

<img src="screenshots/12-tracking.png" width="320" alt="Live tracking screen" />
<img src="screenshots/13-sos.png" width="320" alt="Emergency assistance screen" />

Tracking follows the bus while the trip is running. Position comes from the
assigned driver's phone and only theirs, so the marker cannot jump between two
devices.

<a id="discounts"></a>

<img src="screenshots/14-discount.png" width="320" alt="Fare discount screen for senior, student and PWD verification" />
<img src="screenshots/15-notifications.png" width="320" alt="Notification feed" />

A senior, student or PWD discount needs an uploaded ID and an operator's
approval. Uploading is not approval: the submission sits PENDING and discounts
nothing until somebody reviews it. The IDs live in a private bucket and are
read through short-lived signed URLs.

Everything in the notification feed was written by the server when something
actually happened. The app cannot create one — which is what makes the feed
worth believing.

## 2. Operator — running a company

Every screen here reads through a view that is scoped to the caller's own
operator **inside the view**, never by a filter the caller has to remember.

### Dashboard

<img src="screenshots/20-operator-dashboard.png" width="900" alt="Operator dashboard: today's passengers, revenue, boarded count and trip statuses" />

Today's figures, aggregated server-side. The on-time percentage is `null` until
something has actually departed rather than a confident 100%, and an uncrewed
departure is called out as a warning.

### Schedule

<img src="screenshots/21-operator-schedule.png" width="900" alt="Operator schedule listing departures with coach, crew and status" />

Every departure this company runs, with search, filters, sorting and paging.
A departure with no driver says so in red.

<img src="screenshots/22-schedule-form.png" width="900" alt="The schedule form: route, coach, trip number, date, times and fare" />

The form tells you when the coach and crew come free again — "spoken for until
16:30, 30 minutes after it arrives" — so the next departure can be planned
against it rather than discovered by being refused.

**It does not decide whether the coach is free.** Nothing on the client does.
Two people pressing Save at the same instant would both read "free" and both
write, so the answer comes from an exclusion constraint in the database, and a
clash comes back naming the departure in the way:

> **That departure was refused.** That clashes with CHERRY-CLASH-1, which has
> the same bus or crew at that time.

### Drivers and crew

<img src="screenshots/23-drivers.png" width="900" alt="Driver management showing account status and availability as separate columns" />

The important thing on this screen is that there are **two statuses and they
mean different things**:

| | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| **Account status** | May this person sign in? |
| **Availability** | May they be given a **new** trip? |

A driver on a rest day is **Active + Unavailable**: they open the app, see
their history and their notifications, and are simply not rostered. The single
status this replaced could not express that, which is the ordinary case for
crew.

<img src="screenshots/24-set-unavailable.png" width="900" alt="Set unavailable dialog explaining that access is kept" />

Note what the dialog promises — *"will keep their access and can still see
their trips and notifications"* — and that deactivating the account is a
separate, red button. The action that removes somebody's access should never be
the easy misclick.

Licences are tracked and enforced: a driver whose licence expires before the
departure date cannot be rostered onto it.

### Fleet

<img src="screenshots/25-operator-fleet.png" width="900" alt="Fleet list with coach numbers, plates, seat counts and road status" />

Capacity is set once, when the coach is created, and the seat map is generated
from it in the same transaction — so a bus can never exist with a seat map that
disagrees with how many people it holds. It is not editable afterwards for the
same reason.

"Take off the road" does not cancel that coach's scheduled departures. It stops
new seats being sold on them and stops it being scheduled again; whether each
departure still runs is a decision with passengers attached, made one at a time.

### Travel data, scanner, counter sales

<img src="screenshots/26-travel-data.png" width="900" alt="Per-trip travel data with occupancy and on-time performance" />
<img src="screenshots/27-scanner.png" width="900" alt="QR boarding scanner" />

A scan is judged against **the trip being boarded**, not merely against the
ticket being genuine. A real, paid, correctly signed ticket for tomorrow's bus
is refused at today's door, and the refusal is logged rather than thrown away.
Boarding is recorded per passenger, so a family of four aboard is four people,
not one booking.

<img src="screenshots/28-counter-sale.png" width="900" alt="Assisted booking at the counter" />

Counter sales are for passengers with no smartphone. Cash is the one payment in
this build that is real money: the record names the clerk who took it, and only
an operator manager or an admin can write one.

## 3. Admin — running the platform

### Overview

<img src="screenshots/30-admin-overview.png" width="900" alt="Platform overview with cross-operator totals" />

Platform-wide totals, and the same figures broken out per operator. The admin's
revenue figure for a company is asserted equal to what that company sees on its
own dashboard, so the two consoles cannot drift apart about money.

### Operators — where the hierarchy starts

<img src="screenshots/31-admin-operators.png" width="900" alt="Operator management with view, edit, add login and deactivate actions" />

Only an administrator creates an operator, and only an administrator creates
the login that lets that company into its own console. There is no public
operator registration and no self-service route to one.

<img src="screenshots/32-add-operator-login.png" width="900" alt="Add a login dialog for an operator" />

<img src="screenshots/33-temporary-password.png" width="900" alt="The temporary password, shown once with a warning" />

The password is shown **once** and is stored nowhere readable — if it is lost,
the way back is a reset, not a lookup. Its alphabet leaves out `I l 1 O 0`,
because this gets read aloud across a ticket counter.

Creating an account needs a key that must never reach the app, so it happens in
an Edge Function. That function authorises *before* creating anything, so a
refused request leaves no half-made account behind.

### The temporary password is temporary

<img src="screenshots/38-forced-password-change.png" width="320" alt="Forced password change on first sign-in" />

The new account cannot reach its console until it has chosen a password of its
own. This screenshot was taken by provisioning an account and signing straight
in with the password from the sheet above — it is the real gate, not a mock-up
of one.

### Schedules and crew, platform-wide

<img src="screenshots/34-admin-schedules.png" width="900" alt="Every departure on the platform, filterable by operator" />

This is the view that shows what the operator screens hide: a departure whose
coach has been withdrawn, or whose operator has been deactivated, is called out
rather than filtered away. The turnaround buffer — how long a coach stays
spoken for after it arrives — is tuned from here, and widening it is refused
outright, naming the pair, if it would put two coaches on top of each other.

<img src="screenshots/35-admin-crew.png" width="900" alt="Every driver and conductor across all operators" />

An administrator can see everybody and close a door. The day-to-day of a roster
— hiring, editing, rest days — belongs to the operator who employs them.

### Fleet and routes

<img src="screenshots/36-admin-fleet.png" width="900" alt="Every coach across all operators" />
<img src="screenshots/37-admin-routes.png" width="900" alt="Route management" />

A coach can be moved between companies, but only while it is on nobody's
schedule — otherwise its trips would point at another company's bus.

**Nothing in this console deletes anything.** Operators, terminals, routes,
coaches and departures are all referenced by bookings, payments, tickets and
boarding scans. Every "Delete" is a deactivation, every confirmation dialog
says so, and every change goes through an audited function.

## 4. Driver and crew — at the bus

<img src="screenshots/40-driver-duty.png" width="320" alt="Driver's assigned trips" />

Deliberately separate from the operator console: crew must not see revenue and
fleet. They see their own assignments and nothing else.

<img src="screenshots/41-driver-trip.png" width="320" alt="One trip: status, position sharing, and the trip lifecycle buttons" />

The trip lifecycle — open boarding, start, end — and position sharing, which is
off until the trip starts. Each step is idempotent: a retry after a dropped
connection does not move a timestamp that was already recorded.

Ending a trip is what completes the boarded passengers' bookings and awards
their loyalty points.

<img src="screenshots/42-driver-scan.png" width="320" alt="Door scanner in the crew app" />

The same scanner and the same server checks as the operator's terminal.

## Regenerating these

```bash
pnpm docs:screens
```

With the stack running and the app pointed at **localhost** (the script refuses
to run against anything else — these are pictures of seed data).

It drives a real Chrome over the DevTools Protocol: no Playwright, no
Puppeteer, no browser download. It signs in and clicks through each journey
rather than rendering components in isolation, because a screenshot of a screen
nobody can reach is worse than no screenshot.

The shot list is [`scripts/walkthrough-steps.mjs`](../scripts/walkthrough-steps.mjs),
written to read like this document. A broken step prints `!!` with its reason
and the rest carry on, so one bad selector costs one picture rather than
thirty-six.

```bash
WALKTHROUGH_ONLY=operator pnpm docs:screens
```

narrows a run to one journey while fixing a step.

### What these pictures cannot show

- **Push notifications arriving on a handset.** That needs a real device, a
  development build with push credentials, and a deployed `send-push`. The
  in-app feed is here; the last hop is not.
- **The live map.** MapLibre is native-only — the web build shows the fallback
  panel you can see on the driver's trip screen.
- **The camera.** The boarding scanner needs HTTPS or localhost and a real
  camera; these runs are headless.
